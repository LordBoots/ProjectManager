Condensation of my research:

1. LTD might not be the best way forward with an unknown initial sales volume:
====================================================================================
 An LTD is capper at 28% tax - this seems good but only if you assume a high profit margin. 
 Scenario: 
  Game makes only 60K in revenue @ 28% tax = 16.8K in tax (LTD)
  Game makes 100K in revenue @ 28% tax = 28K in tax (LTD)

  Game makes 60K in revenue @ 17% tax = 10.2K in tax (LTD)
  Game makes 100K in revenue @ 33% tax = 33K in tax (LTD)
 
 If the game were launched under an LTD TODAY and the game only made 60K in revenue, the tax would be 16.8K
 If the game were launched under an LTC TODAY and the game made 60K in revenue, the tax would be 11,020 (this ignores compounding brackets so it's actually less)

 If the game were launched under an LTD TODAY and the game made 100K in revenue, the tax would be 28K
 If the game were launched under an LTC TODAY and the game made 100K in revenue, the tax would be 33K (this ignores compounding brackets so it's actually less)

 So the crossover threshhold for when you want to convert an LTC into an LTD is around 70-78K in revenue.
 Until then it is a waste of money to run under an LTD.

2. Naming the explicit cash injection in the contract could subject the NZ based developer to retroactive tax on the investment.
==========================================================================================================================
 This is a risk that is not worth taking.
 The best way to avoid this is to not name the explicit cash injection in the contract.
 
 The contract would state what it does now but it will remove the explicit cash injection from the contract and will reply on clear revenue split agreement.
 The contract is still FULLY legal without the cash amount listed, the SPLIT agreement is actually the most important part of the contract.
